Agency guides to the minimum budget for Google Ads in India hand you a floor: ₹10,000 a month "is a realistic minimum" in [OneCity's Bangalore guide](https://onecity.co.in/blog/google-ads-cost-in-bangalore/), ₹15,000 a month in [upGrowth's pricing guide](https://upgrowth.in/google-ads-pricing-india-2026/), with rupee-per-click tables beside them. Read Google's own Help pages and the floor disappears. Google's page on managing spend says "The amount of your budget is entirely up to you", and Google's India bidding page answers its own minimum-bid question with "Bidding amounts are ultimately up to you". So Google Ads cost in India starts wherever you set it.

What Google does set is the most it can charge you: two times your average daily budget on any one day, and 30.4 times it in a month, per campaign. Those ceilings are the numbers to plan around, because every rupee-per-click figure in the guides I read is an agency's estimate, and Google's own examples are in pounds. Google's en-IN Help pages quoted here were checked on 4 November 2026; the rupee figures below are my arithmetic on Google's multipliers, and Google can reword those pages at any time.

Google's Help pages set no minimum budget. You choose an average daily budget per campaign, and for most campaigns Google can bill up to twice that on a single day and no more than 30.4 times it in a calendar month. A ₹500 daily budget therefore costs at most ₹1,000 in a day and ₹15,200 in a full month.

## Is There a Minimum Budget for Google Ads in India?

No: none of Google's budget pages I read sets a rupee floor, and the agency figures are advice about a realistic starting spend. Here is what each side actually says, as of November 2026:

|---|---|---|

| [Google India: bidding FAQ](https://business.google.com/in/ad-tools/bidding/) | "Bidding amounts are ultimately up to you" | Google's rule |
| [Google: Choose your bid and budget](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2375454?hl=en-IN) | "Tip: Start small. Check your account daily after applying a new budget" | Google's advice |

The agency floors answer a different question from Google's pages. Google tells you what the system will accept; the agencies tell you what they think a campaign needs to produce enough clicks to learn from. Both can be right at once, as long as you do not read the agency number as a Google requirement.

## Google Ads Cost in India per Day and per Month: The Ceiling Table

Google's [About spending limits page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10486637?hl=en-IN) defines two limits for each campaign. The **daily spending limit** is "the maximum amount that you can be billed for a campaign on a given day", two times the average daily budget for most campaigns. The **monthly spending limit** is the average daily budget multiplied by 30.4, which Google explains as 365 days divided by 12 months.

Applied to rupee budgets, the two multipliers give these ceilings for one campaign:

| Average daily budget you set | Most Google can bill on one day (2x) | Most Google can bill in a full month (30.4x) |
|---|---|---|
| ₹100 | ₹200 | **₹3,040** |
| ₹300 | ₹600 | **₹9,120** |
| ₹500 | ₹1,000 | **₹15,200** |
| ₹1,000 | ₹2,000 | **₹30,400** |
| ₹2,500 | ₹5,000 | **₹76,000** |

Google's en-IN pages give the same rule in pounds: a GBP 10 average daily budget has a GBP 20 daily spending limit and a GBP 304 monthly spending limit. The rupee rows are my arithmetic on that formula, not prices Google lists for India.

What the table does not show:

- **The limits are per campaign.** Three campaigns at ₹500 a day each can, on my arithmetic, bill up to ₹3,000 on one day and ₹45,600 in a month across the account.
- **The ceiling is a maximum, not a forecast.** Google's [About average daily budgets page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6385083?hl=en-IN) says that on some days you might not reach your average daily budget, and on others you might exceed it.
- **"For most campaigns" has exceptions.** The spending-limits page says a "Pay for conversions" campaign has no daily spending limit, though it still stays within 30.4 times the average daily budget for the month.

## How to Work Out a Daily Budget From a Monthly Amount

Google's instruction is to divide the monthly amount by 30.4: its own example turns GBP 304 a month into GBP 10 a day. An average daily budget is entered as a daily figure, so a monthly plan has to be converted first. The rupee rows are my arithmetic:

| Monthly amount you plan | Divide by 30.4 | Daily budget to enter (rounded down) | Monthly limit at that daily budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| ₹10,000 | ₹328.95 | ₹328 | ₹9,971.20 |
| ₹15,000 | ₹493.42 | ₹493 | ₹14,987.20 |
| ₹25,000 | ₹822.37 | ₹822 | ₹24,988.80 |
| ₹50,000 | ₹1,644.74 | ₹1,644 | ₹49,977.60 |
| ₹1,00,000 | ₹3,289.47 | ₹3,289 | ₹99,985.60 |

Dividing by 30 instead of 30.4 is the common slip. ₹15,000 divided by 30 gives ₹500 a day, and ₹500 multiplied by 30.4 is a monthly limit of ₹15,200, so the campaign is allowed to bill ₹200 more than you planned. At ₹50,000 a month the gap is ₹666.67. Rounding the daily figure down keeps the monthly limit at or under your plan.

## Can Google Spend More Than My Daily Budget?

Yes, up to twice the average daily budget on a given day. Google's [Manage your spend page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1704424?hl=en-IN) calls this over-delivery and says it "can help make up for days when traffic is slow". The month still averages out: you are not charged more than 30.4 times the average daily budget.

The rarer case is a campaign whose clicks cost more than a limit. The spending-limits page separates two numbers:

- **Served cost:** the cost of all the clicks or impressions the campaign received.
- **Billed cost:** what you pay after adjustments for items like invalid activity.

When served cost goes past the daily or monthly spending limit, billed cost stops at the limit and Google covers the difference. Google's example is a GBP 10 budget that serves GBP 23 of clicks on one day: you are billed GBP 20 and Google covers GBP 3. Scaled to a ₹500 budget on my arithmetic, that is ₹1,150 served, ₹1,000 billed and ₹150 covered by Google. The Manage your spend page adds that an overdelivery credit is applied to your account if your ad is shown so much that you exceed your monthly charging limit.

To see it in an account, Google's spending-limits page points to the **Billed cost** report in Report editor (Template gallery, then Billing), where Google says to subtract billed cost from served cost to see the difference you were not billed for.

## Mid-Month Starts and Budget Changes: How Google Recalculates

The 30.4x figure assumes a campaign that runs the whole month on one budget. Google's [How budget changes take effect page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10487143?hl=en-IN) and the spending-limits page cover the other cases, and none of the agency guides I read for this post works through them. Google's examples are in pounds; the rupee versions below multiply Google's figures by 100.

| Situation | Google's rule | Rupee example (my arithmetic) |
|---|---|---|
| Campaign starts partway through a month | Only the days the campaign was running count; a new campaign's pacing goal uses the remaining calendar days | Start on 21 November at ₹500 a day: 10 calendar days (21 to 30 November) x ₹500 = ₹5,000 for November on that rule, not ₹15,200 |
| You raise the budget mid-month | Spend for the rest of the month will not exceed the new average daily budget x the remaining calendar days | ₹500 a day from 1 November; ₹10,300 spent by 24 November; raise to ₹1,000: ₹10,300 + (₹1,000 x 7 days) = ₹17,300 for November |
| You cut the budget mid-month | Same formula with the lower budget; that day's daily limit still uses the higher budget | ₹1,000 a day; ₹27,000 spent with 5 days left; cut to ₹500: ₹27,000 + (₹500 x 5) = ₹29,500, with a ₹2,000 limit on the day of the edit and ₹1,000 after |
| Several edits on one day | The daily limit uses the highest budget you set that day; the monthly limit uses the latest | Raise ₹1,000 to ₹5,000, then cut to ₹500 the same day: that day's limit is ₹10,000 |
| Ad schedule runs only some days | The monthly limit stays 30.4x, and Google paces to reach it regardless of how many days are scheduled | A weekday-only ₹500 campaign in November 2026 (21 weekdays) can still bill ₹15,200, about ₹724 per weekday, inside the ₹1,000 daily limit |
| You change the campaign end date | Counted as a budget change for the monthly maximum | Re-check the month's limit after any end-date edit |

The ad-schedule row matters for small Indian businesses that only want ads during office hours or on weekdays: switching days off does not shrink the month's maximum, it concentrates the same 30.4x into fewer days.

### Campaign Total Budgets Work Differently

A campaign total budget sets one amount for a fixed run instead of a daily average. Google's [About campaign total budgets page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10486938?hl=en-IN) and the budget-changes page describe it this way, as of November 2026:

- It suits a time-bound event of 3 to 90 days on Search, Shopping and Performance Max (Demand Gen and YouTube can run up to a year).
- There is no daily spending limit, and "you will never be charged more than your total budget". The budget-changes page calls it "a hard cap for the total duration of the campaign".
- Google re-paces spend daily to use the remaining budget over the remaining days, so a ₹10,000 total over 10 days starts near ₹1,000 a day on my arithmetic and moves from there.
- It is available only when you create a new campaign; an existing campaign cannot switch from an average daily budget.

### Shared Budgets

A shared budget, per Google's [Choose your bid and budget page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2375454?hl=en-IN), sets one amount across multiple campaigns, so a campaign that underspends on a given day leaves room for another to use. The same page notes shared budgets do not work with campaigns in a campaign group or an experiment. Once spend is split across several campaigns, a consistent [campaign naming convention](/blog/campaign-naming-convention/) is what keeps the budget report readable.

## Google Ads Cost per Click in India: Why There Is No Official Rupee Figure

Each click is priced in an auction, and none of the Google pages cited here gives a fixed rupee CPC. Google's [Choose your bid and budget page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2375454?hl=en-IN) says the auction "uses both quality and bid" to decide position, and that "you'll only pay what's minimally required to hold your Ad Rank". That is the same pricing the [growth vs performance marketing breakdown](/blog/growth-marketing-vs-performance-marketing/) describes for paid media in general: inventory bought in real-time auctions on CPC, CPM or CPA terms.

What you control per click, from Google's pages:

1. **A maximum CPC bid.** Google's [Cost per click page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/116495?hl=en-IN) says a click "won't cost you more than the maximum CPC bid amount that you set", and that you'll often be charged less. The exception it names is bid adjustments or Enhanced CPC.
2. **Your bid inputs.** Google's [India bidding page](https://business.google.com/in/ad-tools/bidding/) says the amount depends on your campaign type, how much your keywords cost and how successful your keywords are, and suggests setting a maximum bid if you are not sure where to start.
3. **A cost estimate before you spend.** Google's [Keyword Planner page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243?hl=en-IN) lists "View the average cost for your ad to show on searches for a keyword" as a benefit, and says that to access Keyword Planner "You must complete your account setup by entering your billing information."

The rupee CPC ranges you will find online are agency estimates. Two of them, as of November 2026:

| Agency guide | Rupee CPC figure | Basis the page gives |
|---|---|---|
| [Mega Digital](https://megadigital.ai/en/blog/google-ads-cost-in-india/) (updated 5 June 2026) | Search average CPC ₹20 to ₹150; Display average CPC about ₹5 | Cites Willis Writing benchmarks for Search CPCs above ₹100 in competitive industries, and Exchange4Media for Display costs of ₹5 to ₹10 |
| [upGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/google-ads-pricing-india-2026/) (updated 16 September 2026) | ₹25 to ₹60 per click for most advertisers outside finance, insurance and B2B SaaS | "upGrowth estimates from September 2026" |

Treat either range as a planning guess for your own keywords. The Keyword Planner estimate for your keywords and locations is the closer number, and it sits behind the billing step.

## Google Ads Cost in India Beyond the Budget: Fees and Tax

The budget you set covers media: the clicks or impressions Google bills. Mega Digital's guide separates the two kinds of expense as the fee for managing PPC and the cost of advertising. An agency's management fee is a separate line on top of media, and [what Indian agencies charge for SEO](/blog/seo-cost-in-india/) shows how widely retainer prices vary in the same market.

These cost lines sit outside the budget rules:

- **Tax.** Agency guides add tax on top of ad spend (Mega Digital's says 18%), so check the tax line on your invoice separately from the budget rules here.
- **TDS.** If your business deducts TDS on payments to Google, the [Google Ads TDS refund guide](/blog/google-ads-tds-refund/) covers how Google's India page handles it.
- **How you pay.** Whether that spend is charged to a card after ads run or paid ahead by UPI or NetBanking depends on the payment setting, and [Google Ads payment methods in India](/blog/google-ads-payment-methods-india/) maps each method to postpay and prepay.
- **Promotional credit.** New-advertiser offers run on their own spend-and-time conditions; the [Google Ads promotional code guide](/blog/google-ads-promotional-code-india/) has them.

After the money is spent, Performance Max and other multi-network campaign types do not report under Paid Search in GA4; they get [their own Cross Network channel](/blog/what-is-cross-network-in-google-analytics/), which matters when you compare spend against results.

## Setting Your First Google Ads Budget in India

Working only from Google's own rules, a first budget comes down to these decisions:

1. **Pick the monthly amount you can afford to spend with nothing to show for it.** Google's own tip is "Start small", so treat the first month as a test of your keywords and ads.
2. **Divide by 30.4 and round down.** ₹15,000 becomes ₹493 a day, with a ₹14,987.20 monthly limit.
3. **Check the 2x day.** At ₹493 a day, one campaign can bill up to ₹986 on a busy day. If that would hurt your cash flow, lower the budget.
4. **Use a campaign total budget for a fixed event.** A 3 to 90 day sale gets a hard cap and no daily limit, but only in a new campaign.
5. **Set a maximum CPC if you want a per-click ceiling**, then look at the Billed cost report after the first month to see whether served cost ever went past a limit.

Google Ads cost in India is a number you choose, and the rules worth learning are the 2x day, the 30.4x month and the mid-month recalculation. Put the agency's rupee-per-click range aside until Keyword Planner shows you an estimate for your own keywords, and size the month to a daily budget you would accept being billed twice in one day.
