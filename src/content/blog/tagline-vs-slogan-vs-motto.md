"Ads are coming to AI. But not to Claude." ran under Anthropic's Super Bowl ads in February 2026, and a spot from that campaign won the Super Clio. So which is it: a tagline, a slogan or a motto? Put it through the tagline vs slogan vs motto test that Brand Vision publishes, and it comes out a slogan.

I classify a line by its record rather than by how it sounds: where it ran, who it was written for, and whether it was still there a year later. On that record, Anthropic's tagline is a plainer sentence that has headed its homepage since at least October 2023, and the Super Bowl line is a slogan standing on a written commitment. Twelve B2B software homepages, compared across four years further down, show how few hero lines keep that record for long.

## Tagline vs Slogan vs Motto: The Definitions

A tagline is the line a brand keeps beside its name for years. A slogan is written for one campaign, offer or launch and retires when it ends. A motto is a guiding principle written for the people inside an organization, which customers may never see.

| | Tagline | Slogan | Motto |
|---|---|---|---|
| Written for | Customers and the market | Customers, during one campaign | Employees and members |
| Lifespan | Years, until a rebrand | One campaign, offer or launch | As long as the values hold |
| Where it appears | Beside the logo, in the homepage hero, in the title tag | Ads, end cards, launch pages, sales collateral | Values pages, codes of conduct, careers pages |
| Question it answers | Who is this brand? | Why pay attention now? | How do we behave? |
| AI or B2B example | Anthropic: "AI research and products that put safety at the frontier" | Anthropic: "Ads are coming to AI. But not to Claude." | Google: "don't be evil" |

Every example in the table is checked and dated in the classification table further down. A tagline also sits above the messaging work: [how a tagline differs from a messaging pillar](/blog/messaging-pillars/) is covered in the pillars guide.

## Is a Tagline the Same as a Slogan?

In brand strategy, no: a tagline outlasts campaigns and a slogan belongs to one. In everyday English and in the ad trade the words overlap, as these sources show:

- Merriam-Webster's entry for [slogan](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/slogan) gives "a brief attention-getting phrase used in advertising or promotion" as one sense, lists motto among its synonyms, and traces the word to the Scottish Gaelic sluagh-ghairm, an army's cry, with a first known use in 1513.
- [Marketing Dive's review of the first half of 2026](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/best-brand-campaigns-h1-2026-sold-honesty-consumers-buying/819960/) introduces Anthropic's Super Bowl line with "reads a tagline", using the word in its ad-trade sense, for a line inside one campaign's ads. On this page, tagline means the line a brand keeps.
- Marketing Dive's [September 2026 report on Staples](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/staples-cmo-on-introducing-a-hard-button-amid-bigger-b2b-push/830611/) calls "That was easy!" a "catchphrase", a fourth word for a line Marketing Dive says TV commercials made recognizable.
- GitHub's 1 October 2022 homepage markup (the capture linked in the table below) put the rotating words after "Let's build from here" in a CSS class named `home-tagline-appendix`, so its developers called the hero line a tagline.

The practical habit: when a colleague asks for a "tagline", ask which of the three jobs they mean before you write anything.

## Brand Vision's Test and Who Owns Taglines and Slogans

Brand Vision's [guide to taglines, slogans and mottos](https://www.brandvm.com/post/brand-tagline-slogan-motto-difference) (Dana Nemirovsky, February 2026) publishes a four-rule test, quoted here in full:

1. "Is the line meant to identify the brand across years and channels? Choose a brand tagline."
2. "Is the line meant to support one campaign, offer, or launch? Choose a brand slogan."
3. "Is the line meant to guide internal decisions and standards? Choose a brand motto."
4. "If more than one answer is yes, write more than one line."

Ownership is published too. Stephen Houraghan's [tagline vs slogan guide](https://brandmasteracademy.com/tagline-vs-slogan/) at Brand Master Academy says the brand strategist "develops a tagline in the initial stages of the brand strategy", while "the marketing team, overseen by the brand manager and nearly always working with copywriters, develops a slogan during the creative stages of a marketing campaign." The same guide describes how a slogan becomes a tagline: the audience adopts it first, and the brand often "adopts the highly popular slogan in place of its original tagline".

The test sorts clean cases. The harder lines land on its fourth rule: a campaign line can carry a lasting commitment, and a homepage hero line can last one product cycle. For those I add two checks of my own:

1. **Record check.** Open the line's page in the Wayback Machine a year after launch. A line still beside the name is doing a tagline's job, whatever the brand guide calls it.
2. **Source check.** If the line restates a commitment published on a permanent page, such as a policy post or a values page, label the line by where it runs and track the commitment separately. The line can retire while the commitment holds.

## The Edge Case: Anthropic's Super Bowl Line

Here is "Ads are coming to AI. But not to Claude." against Brand Vision's questions:

| Question | Answer | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Identifies the brand across years and channels? | Not yet | It ran with one campaign, the February 2026 Super Bowl ads, per the Marketing Dive review linked above |
| Supports one campaign? | Yes | Marketing Dive quotes it as the line in those ads; the campaign's spot won the 2026 Super Clio ([the Clios](https://clios.com/super-clio/super-clio-2026/)) |
| Guides internal decisions? | The commitment behind it does | "Claude will remain ad-free" ([Anthropic, 4 February 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-is-a-space-to-think)) |

Against my checks:

- Record check: [Anthropic's homepage](https://www.anthropic.com/) on 3 October 2026 opens with "AI research and products that put safety at the frontier", the same hero line as the Wayback Machine captures from [2 October 2023](https://web.archive.org/web/20231002203115/https://www.anthropic.com/), [2 October 2024](https://web.archive.org/web/20241002012204/https://www.anthropic.com/) and [1 October 2025](https://web.archive.org/web/20251001215922/https://www.anthropic.com/). That sentence passes; the Super Bowl line has not had its year yet.
- Source check: the ad-free commitment has its own page, which adds "Should we need to revisit this approach, we'll be transparent about our reasons for doing so." The slogan can come off the air while the commitment stays the thing users hold Anthropic to.

The ads sold Claude, the product brand, and claude.com has its own hero line: "Think fast, build faster" in the [Wayback Machine capture of 1 October 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20261001070114/https://claude.com/) and on the live page on 3 October 2026. That line has not had a record check here; the dated tagline in this post is the company's.

My verdict: a slogan, with the commitment underneath it and the company's tagline on anthropic.com. The campaign's dates and sources are in my roundup of [recent innovative marketing examples](/blog/recent-innovative-marketing-examples/), where it is one of the B2B entries. If Anthropic keeps the line beside the Claude name for years, it will have made the slogan-to-tagline move Brand Master Academy describes, and the record check will show it.

What the 12 rows show:

- **0 of 12** October 2022 hero lines appeared anywhere in the October 2026 HTML of the same site.
- **2 of 12** were still the hero line a year later, in the October 2023 captures: Mailchimp and GitHub. HubSpot's had moved down into the subhead, "Software that's powerful, not overpowering."
- **7 of 12** October 2026 hero lines name AI, agents or "agentic": Slack, Salesforce, Notion, Zapier, Linear, Asana and Intercom.
- Claims outlived the wording. Salesforce's 2022 page described "the world's #1 CRM", and its 2026 US title tag reads "Salesforce: The #1 Agentic AI CRM"; Stripe's hero line kept "infrastructure" while "Payments" became "Financial".

None of the 12 lasted four years, but Mailchimp's and GitHub's lines were still in place a year later, so by the record check they did a tagline's job for at least a year before they were replaced. A hero line can earn tagline status and still lose it, so file it in the brand guide with a review date rather than as permanent. I picked these 12 as well-known B2B software brands, so read the result as a pattern in that group, not a rate for the whole market.

## Tagline vs Motto: Lines Written for the People Inside

A motto can be public and still be a motto, as long as it is written for the people inside.

- Google's [Code of Conduct](https://abc.xyz/investor/board-and-governance/google-code-of-conduct/), last updated 16 July 2026, says it expects "all Googlers to be guided by both the letter and the spirit of this Code" and ends: "And remember... don't be evil, and if you see something that you think isn't right - speak up!" The phrase appears once in the document.
- Atlassian's [values page](https://www.atlassian.com/company/values) says its five values "guide what we do, why we create, and who we hire", and that "these five values remain constant". The first is "Open company, no bullshit"; the others include "Don't #@!% the customer" and "Play, as a team".

Atlassian shows the split between a buyer-facing line and a motto inside one company. Its [homepage hero](https://web.archive.org/web/20261003071959/https://www.atlassian.com/) on 3 October 2026, "Unleash your teams and their agents", speaks to buyers about the product. "Open company, no bullshit" speaks to the people deciding how to work there, and a buyer could miss it without losing anything.

## Tagline vs Slogan vs Motto: Read the Record

Anthropic's three lines show the method: the company tagline on anthropic.com is in every yearly capture since 2023, the Super Bowl line has not had its year, and the ad-free commitment is tracked on its own page. Your own tagline vs slogan vs motto calls take the same evidence. For a line already live, look it up in the Wayback Machine before it goes into the brand guide, and if it was gone a year after launch, file it as a slogan or a positioning headline with an end date. For a new line, set a review date a year after launch. Start with your homepage's hero line.
