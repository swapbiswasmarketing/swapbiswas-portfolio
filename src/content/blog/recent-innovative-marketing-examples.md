A roundup of recent innovative marketing examples can carry this year's date and still feature campaigns from nearly three years earlier. <a href="https://www.dharveymedia.com/7-best-digital-campaigns-that-will-shape-2026-marketing/" rel="nofollow noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">A post published on January 5, 2026</a>, titled "7 Best Digital Campaigns That Will Shape 2026 Marketing", lists General Motors and Netflix's Will Ferrell electric-vehicle ad under the heading "7 Best Digital Campaigns That Shaped 2025 Marketing". That spot [ran during the Super Bowl in February 2023](https://deadline.com/2023/02/will-ferrell-travels-through-stranger-things-bridgerton-squid-game-worlds-general-motors-netflix-super-bowl-commercial-1235257432/).

So this list applies one filter before anything else. A campaign is in only if a trade or news page I read dates its launch to January 2025 or later, and that page is linked on its row. The 15 below all pass it, and I grouped them by the mechanic that did the work, because the mechanic is the part you can reuse. Each one ends with the move a B2B or SaaS team can take from it. Launch months and results are as of September 2026.

## Recent Innovative Marketing Examples at a Glance

Each line gives the campaign and its launch month, then my one-line read on why it stands out:

1. **Dove, #ShareTheFirst (June 2025):** asked people to post their first selfie attempt rather than the best of dozens, with more than 100 creators going first.
2. **Expedia and IShowSpeed (April 2026):** the brand became the travel partner inside a streamer's own format, a 12-hour livestream across five countries.
3. **Duolingo, the death of Duo (February 2025):** reviving the mascot took a language lesson, so joining the story meant using the app.
4. **Olipop, the 5-cent PR box (June 2025):** the influencer unboxing parcel went to ordinary Amazon shoppers, capped at 5,000 boxes.
5. **KFC, "Obsession" (July 2025):** brought back the founder-mascot with a scowl, using the brand's oldest asset to signal a harder edge.
6. **Burger King, "There's A New King And It's You" (March 2026):** retiring the mascot while admitting the category's slump on Oscars night made the promised fix easier to believe.
7. **Kalshi, the NBA Finals AI ad (June 2025):** made with Google's Veo 3 for roughly $2,000, on a brief that asked for chaos.
8. **Dr Pepper, "It's a Pepper Fling" (May 2026):** a four-episode social microdrama turned a limited-time flavor into a cliff-hanger romance.
9. **American Eagle, "Sydney Sweeney Has Great Jeans" (July 2025):** put Sweeney in the brand's core jeans, from a Snapchat experience to the Las Vegas Sphere, under a double-meaning headline.

## What Makes a Marketing Campaign Innovative?

A campaign is innovative when something about it is new for its category: the way people take part, the way the ad is made, or the position it takes in a familiar slot. The 15 on this list sort into those three kinds, and each kind has its own test:

| Kind of new | Campaigns on this list | The test |
|---|---|---|
| A new way to take part | Dove, Expedia, Duolingo, Olipop, e.l.f., Slack | Does the action prove the product's point? Duolingo's revival lessons did; a generic sweepstakes would not. |
| A new way to make the ad | Kalshi, Dr Pepper | Does the method or format change what the ad costs or how people watch it? |
| A new position in a familiar slot | KFC, Burger King, American Eagle, Degree, Astronomer, Anthropic, Staples | Could a competitor run the same line? If so, it is a refresh. |

## Creator-Led Campaigns: Dove and Expedia

### 1. Dove: #ShareTheFirst (June 2025)

Launched June 2025 with agency Edelman, using global creators and UK out-of-home, per [Marketing Dive's 2025 review](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/best-marketing-campaigns-2025-navigating-uncertainty/807155/).

- What they did: Dove built this chapter of its "Real Beauty" platform entirely from creator content, with no extra studio work. The prompt was to post the first photo you take of yourself, answering research behind the campaign that found nine in 10 surveyed women take up to 50 photos before posting. More than 100 creators started it, and 64 placements at London's Liverpool Street station showed creator content with a scrollable camera-roll element.
- Why it stands out (my read): the ask took seconds and needed no explanation, because it was the Real Beauty idea reduced to one action.
- Result the source states: **over 1 billion impressions** and **94% positive sentiment**.
- Steal this for B2B: swap the studio shoot for a one-post prompt your users can answer from their own screen, such as the first thing they built with your product.

### 2. Expedia and IShowSpeed (April 2026)

Kicked off April 2026 as a global partnership and extended in June, per [Marketing Dive's first-half 2026 review](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/best-brand-campaigns-h1-2026-sold-honesty-consumers-buying/819960/).

- What they did: the streamer IShowSpeed, a creator with 150 million followers, named Expedia his official travel partner. Phase one was a 12-hour livestream that followed him across five countries in a single day, a branded hub called "Exspeedia" and a sweepstakes. In June, Expedia added a shoppable travel experience that connected the content with commerce.
- Why it stands out (my read): Expedia left the show to the streamer, which Thinkingbox's Cam Boyd described in Marketing Dive's review as "enabling him to do his thing," then put a purchase path next to it. My take as a PMM: the purchase path is the part a software company can copy without a 150-million-follower creator.
- Result the source states: the livestream **reached more than 400 million people across social**, and Marketing Dive says it helped spur surges in search demand for the island destinations he visited.
- Steal this for B2B: sponsor a practitioner's existing newsletter or livestream in its own format and add one trial or demo path, instead of scripting the creator into an ad.

## Product as the Media: Duolingo and Olipop

### 3. Duolingo: The Death of Duo (February 2025)

Ran in February 2025 across Duolingo's global audience, per [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/brand-marketing/duolingo-duo-owl-marketing-strategy/).

- What they did: Duolingo killed off its owl mascot, then spent several days dropping clues about what happened. It staged a funeral on TikTok, and CEO Luis von Ahn read a eulogy on YouTube. Within the week, it asked users to take a language lesson to help bring Duo back, with a dedicated site showing which countries were contributing.
- Why it stands out (my read): the route to the happy ending ran through the product. Every "save Duo" action was a lesson completed.
- Result the source states: the **TikTok funeral was watched by 66 million people**.
- Steal this for B2B: tie the campaign payoff to one core product action, so taking part and activating are the same event. The in-product loops behind that idea are covered in these [product-led growth examples](/blog/product-led-growth-examples/).

### 4. Olipop: The 5-Cent PR Box (June 2025)

Ran in June 2025 in the US, through Amazon, per [Marketing Dive's 2025 review](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/best-marketing-campaigns-2025-navigating-uncertainty/807155/).

- What they did: with Amazon Ads, Olipop dropped 5,000 influencer-style PR boxes on Amazon for 5 cents each, filled with seasonal flavors and branded merchandise.
- Why it stands out (my read): it handed ordinary customers the parcel usually reserved for creators, and the price turned a purchase into an event. Marketing Dive notes it also read as a dig at rival Poppi, whose Super Bowl effort of sending branded vending machines to popular creators drew backlash, though Olipop's director of media and partnerships, Steven Vigilante, said "it was not intentional."
- Result the source states: **35,000 people** signed up to be notified, and the boxes **sold out in two minutes**.
- Steal this for B2B: software companies have their own version of the PR box in the early-access invite. Open a small, capped batch to anyone who signs up, and count sign-ups against supply.

## Mascot and Heritage Resets: KFC and Burger King

### 5. KFC: "Obsession" (July 2025)

Launched July 2025 in the US with agency Highdive, per [Marketing Dive's 2025 review](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/best-marketing-campaigns-2025-navigating-uncertainty/807155/).

- What they did: the "Obsession" spot retold the path Colonel Sanders took to create KFC, and a revamped logo swapped his usual grin for a scowl.
- Why it stands out (my read): it reused the Colonel, which Marketing Dive calls KFC's most iconic brand asset. Highdive co-founder Chad Broude said the gritty approach "signaled to consumers that KFC was serious about reclaiming its spot at the top of the category." My read: a founder's story is an asset a rival cannot copy, so the reset needed no new product claim.
- Result the source states: System1 gave "Obsession" a **4.7-Star Rating against a 3.4-Star average for QSR brands**, and its Brand Fluency score also beat the norm, per [System1's July 30, 2025 review](https://system1group.com/ad-of-the-week/the-colonels-on-the-comeback-trail-and-audiences-love-it).
- Steal this for B2B: before inventing a new brand character, check whether the founder's reason for starting the company already carries your positioning. Your [brand guidelines](/blog/what-are-brand-guidelines/) should say which assets can flex this far.

### 6. Burger King: "There's A New King And It's You" (March 2026)

Launched March 2026 in the US with an ad during the Oscars, made with agency OKRP, per [Marketing Dive's first-half 2026 review](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/best-brand-campaigns-h1-2026-sold-honesty-consumers-buying/819960/).

- What they did: Burger King moved on from its long-time mascot, often deemed creepy, to put customers at the center of its marketing. The Oscars ad acknowledged that "fast food just fell off" and said what the brand was doing to get back on track.
- Why it stands out (my read): in Marketing Dive's review, Method1's Allison Arling-Giorgi called it an example of the pratfall effect: owning a flaw can make people more willing to choose you. My read: retiring the mascot and naming the problem in the same ad made the change look like a response to customers rather than a design refresh.
- Result the source states: Marketing Dive reports **US comparable sales accelerated 5.8% in Q1 2026**, and 97% of franchisees voted to keep an elevated ad fund contribution. Neither number isolates the campaign, though parent company RBI's CEO called the launch of its brand elevation campaign a key highlight of the quarter.
- Steal this for B2B: open a rebrand the same way, by naming what customers said was wrong before showing the change. The sequencing is in this [rebranding rollout plan](/blog/rebranding-rollout-plan/).

## New Ad Formats: Kalshi's AI Spot and Dr Pepper's Microdrama

### 7. Kalshi: The NBA Finals AI Ad (June 2025)

Aired in June 2025 during Game 3 of the NBA Finals in the US, per [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/ai-prompts-used-to-make-the-nba-finals-kalshi-ad-2025-6).

- What they did: Kalshi, which Business Insider describes as a sports betting marketplace, ran a 30-second string of surreal scenes, such as a farmer floating in a pool of eggs, ending on the line "The world's gone mad, trade it." Self-described AI filmmaker PJ Accetturo used Google's Gemini to generate shot lists and prompts, then made the footage with Veo 3.
- Why it stands out (my read): Kalshi asked for "the most unhinged NBA Finals commercial possible," in Accetturo's words, so the oddness of AI video read as a style choice rather than a flaw.
- Result the source states: production numbers. A Kalshi spokesperson said the ad went from idea to live in three days and cost roughly $2,000, and Accetturo said it took 300 to 400 generations to get 15 usable clips. The only performance figure on the page is the spokesperson's forecast of impressions, left out here because it is a projection.
- Steal this for B2B: budget AI video in generations, not shoot days. Fifteen keepers from 300 to 400 generations is a keep rate between 3.75% and 5%, so plan the review time, and save AI video for briefs where strangeness helps, such as event teasers.

### 8. Dr Pepper: "It's a Pepper Fling" (May 2026)

The first episode went live on May 15, 2026, on social media, per [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/dr-pepper-taps-into-microdrama-trend-for-creamy-coconut-return/820499/).

- What they did: Dr Pepper told a four-episode microdrama on social media to promote the limited-time return of its Creamy Coconut flavor. The title plays on the "It's a Pepper Thing" tagline, and the story casts a can of Creamy Coconut as the star of a fleeting love affair. In episode one, a young woman falls for the soda at first sight, then faints when she learns it is only available for a limited time. The series was made with agency Deutsch, with new episodes set to drop weekly on cliff-hangers.
- Why it stands out (my read): the limited-time offer is the plot, so every episode repeats the deadline without reading like a promotion.
- Result the source states: none for the series. The idea came from social listening: since January, talk about the flavor's return had generated **over 6.3 million social media impressions**, per data the brand shared, which is demand measured before launch rather than a campaign result.
- Steal this for B2B: when a feature has a deadline, such as a closing beta or a price change, tell it as a short weekly series where the deadline drives the story.

## Real-World Spectacle: A Sphere Takeover, a Cause Kiosk and Renamed Stations

### 9. American Eagle: "Sydney Sweeney Has Great Jeans" (July 2025)

Unveiled on July 23, 2025, in the US, per [Marketing Dive's launch report](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/why-american-eagle-tapped-sydney-sweeney-for-its-priciest-campaign-yet/753660/); results from its [2025 review](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/best-marketing-campaigns-2025-navigating-uncertainty/807155/).

- What they did: put Sydney Sweeney in American Eagle's core product across social media, an AI-powered Snapchat experience and out-of-home displays, including a takeover of the Sphere in Las Vegas. Criticism arrived almost at once, with some arguing the ads promoted the long-disproven theory of eugenics, and CMO Craig Brommers said the brand stayed the course despite the backlash.
- Why it stands out (my read): a headline that reads two ways gets repeated and argued over, and she was wearing the core product while that happened.
- Result the source states: **nearly 1 million new customers** between late July and early September.
- Steal this for B2B: run a double-meaning line past people outside the marketing team first, because they will find the reading you did not intend. These [message testing examples](/blog/message-testing-examples/) show how to score the reactions.

### 10. e.l.f.: "Give an e.l.f." (October 2025)

Launched in October 2025 in the US, per [Marketing Dive's 2025 review](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/best-marketing-campaigns-2025-navigating-uncertainty/807155/).

- What they did: e.l.f. built the campaign around its annual Impact Report. At a New York City event, visitors picked a cause they cared about at a custom kiosk, and e.l.f. donated to a nonprofit working on it.
- Why it stands out (my read): a report is something a brand publishes, while a kiosk choice is something a person does, and the donation made each choice count.
- Result the source states: **around 195 million earned impressions** and **99% positive sentiment**, according to Chief Brand Officer Laurie Lam.
- Steal this for B2B: B2B teams publish reports too, from benchmark studies to annual surveys. Give readers one choice to make with the data at your event or on the page; a report with a stance is closer to [thought leadership content](/blog/thought-leadership-content/) than to a PDF.

### 11. Degree: The Subway Station Takeover (July 2026)

Launched in July 2026 in New York City subway stations, per [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/degree-sweats-it-out-with-subway-commuters-for-new-york-summer-campaign/826407/).

- What they did: Degree partnered with the Metropolitan Transportation Authority to take over several high-traffic stations and rename them for the heat. West 4th Street became "Sweat Central," Union Square the "Boiler Room" and Herald Square South the "Molten Core," and turnstile signs called Degree "The Official Deodorant of the Hottest Commutes." The idea came from commuter conversations the brand noticed on sites like Reddit. Marketing Dive also reported an August extension: limited-edition sticks, co-branded with the MTA and redeemable for a free subway ride.
- Why it stands out (my read): the station names borrow commuters' complaints, which Degree's head of marketing Chris Symmes described as "using people's own words," and the free-ride stick ties the sample to the commute the ads were about.
- Result the source states: none. Marketing Dive covered the campaign mid-run.
- Steal this for B2B: name the pain in the words your buyers already use in forums and reviews, and put that name where the pain happens, such as the error screen or the release notes.

## Innovative B2B Marketing Campaign Examples From 2025-2026

Four of the 15 come from companies that sell to businesses. Astronomer, Slack and Anthropic borrowed a consumer-sized moment, a viral scandal once and the Super Bowl twice, while Staples aimed its campaign straight at business decision-makers and frontline workers. None is an account-based play. For those, the [ABM campaign examples](/blog/abm-campaign-examples/) cover one-to-one through one-to-many plays.

### 12. Astronomer: The Temporary Spokesperson (July 2025)

Released online in July 2025, per [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/26/astronomer-winks-at-viral-notoriety-with-temporary-spokesperson-gwyneth-paltrow/).

- What they did: after more than a week of headlines over a viral concert kiss-cam clip, the data operations startup posted a video in which Gwyneth Paltrow says she has been "hired on a very temporary basis to speak on behalf of the 300-plus employees at Astronomer." Half-typed questions about the scandal flash up, and she stays on message: "We've been thrilled so many people have a newfound interest in data workflow automation." She plugs the company's upcoming conference and signs off.
- Why it stands out (my read): one wink at the reason everyone was watching, then every line pointed at the product category and an upcoming conference.
- Result the source states: none. TechCrunch reports no view or pipeline numbers.
- Steal this for B2B: keep a pre-approved response for attention you did not ask for, naming the spokesperson and the offer that attention gets pointed at.

### 13. Slack and MrBeast: The Million Dollar Puzzle (February 2026)

Aired on February 8, 2026, during the Super Bowl in the US, per [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/2026/02/08/mrbeast-million-dollar-puzzle-super-bowl-ad/88553887007/).

- What they did: MrBeast announces he has put $1 million in a vault for whoever is first to solve a series of puzzles, with potential clues flashing through the half-minute spot. Viewers went to a MrBeast page on the Salesforce website to register and keep hunting. The ad pitches Slackbot, the AI assistant built into Slack, and it began with a December 29 post from MrBeast about a Super Bowl idea that Salesforce CEO Marc Benioff answered.
- Why it stands out (my read): the 30 seconds were the entry point to a contest that ran for weeks, so the spend led to a registration page instead of ending at one impression.
- Result the source states: no entry counts. Benioff credits Slack with letting the teams "collaborate with MrBeast in 27 days instead of six months," and the [official puzzle page](https://mrbeast.salesforce.com/) now reads "We have a winner!" and says the game is closed.
- Steal this for B2B: put a registration step behind every expensive moment, and tag it so the sign-ups trace back to the spend. A [campaign naming convention](/blog/campaign-naming-convention/) makes that tagging consistent.

### 14. Anthropic: "Ads are coming to AI. But not to Claude." (February 2026)

Aired during the Super Bowl on February 8, 2026, in the US, made with agency Mother, per [Marketing Dive's first-half 2026 review](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/best-brand-campaigns-h1-2026-sold-honesty-consumers-buying/819960/), with the game date from [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/entertainment/2026/02/08/mrbeast-million-dollar-puzzle-super-bowl-ad/88553887007/).

- What they did: Super Bowl ads, including one titled "Can I get a six pack quickly?", showed characters using AI the way most people use it, under the slogan "Ads are coming to AI. But not to Claude."
- Why it stands out (my read): Marketing Dive credits the spot with "a competitive angle that positioned Claude against its ad-curious competitors." My read: the line names something Claude will not do, a commitment Anthropic states on its own [Claude is a space to think](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-is-a-space-to-think) page (February 2026). Any user can check it, and that makes it harder to wave away than a quality claim. By the brand-strategy split, the line is a slogan for one campaign resting on a standing ad-free promise, and [the record check I ran on Anthropic's lines](/blog/tagline-vs-slogan-vs-motto/) sets it against the homepage line the company has kept since at least October 2023.
- Result the source states: the **Super Clio award** for the most creative commercial during the game, confirmed on the [Clios' Super Clio 2026 page](https://clios.com/super-clio/super-clio-2026/).
- Steal this for B2B: write one positioning line around a thing you refuse to do that a competitor does. It only works if the refusal is real and the buyer cares, which is the test any [product positioning](/blog/product-positioning/) statement has to pass.

### 15. Staples: "Hard vs. Easy" (September 2026)

Debuted in September 2026 in the US, per [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/staples-cmo-on-introducing-a-hard-button-amid-bigger-b2b-push/830611/).

- What they did: Staples gave its red Easy Button a black Hard Button alter ego. Two ads show managers stuck with problems such as running out of restroom supplies or a misprinted store sign, and contrast those Hard outcomes with working with Staples. The spots run on YouTube, Hulu and Netflix, with shorter cuts on LinkedIn and Meta, and the wider plan adds elevator placements in corporate buildings and "Hard vs. Easy" collateral for Staples' sales teams. Business decision-makers and frontline workers are the target audiences.
- Why it stands out (my read): CMO Bob Sherwin said, "We don't have an awareness problem, we have a perception problem." My read: the Hard Button uses an asset everyone already knows to show services people do not link to Staples, and the same contrast goes into the sales team's collateral.
- Result the source states: none. Marketing Dive reported it in launch week.
- Steal this for B2B: flip your best-known asset to show the cost of doing things the hard way, and give sales the same contrast as collateral so the ad and the pitch match.

## How to Steal the Mechanic for a B2B or SaaS Launch

Each mechanic is a delivery choice for a message you already have. Pick the message first, then the mechanic that makes people act on it.

| Mechanic | Example on this list | The B2B or SaaS version | What to count |
|---|---|---|---|
| Creator-led | Dove, Expedia | Sponsor a practitioner's existing show or newsletter and add one trial path | Trials or demos from that creator's tagged links |
| Product as the media | Duolingo, Olipop | Make the payoff a product action, or open a capped early-access drop | Activation rate of campaign sign-ups; sign-ups per available slot |
| Mascot and heritage | KFC, Burger King | Retell the founder's reason for starting, or retire a tired asset and say why | Branded search; how often reps repeat the new line |
| New ad formats | Kalshi, Dr Pepper | Make concept tests with AI video, or tell a deadline as a short weekly series | Cost per usable clip; completion rate per episode |
| Real-world spectacle | American Eagle, e.l.f., Degree | Turn a report into a choice people make at your booth, or name the pain where it happens | Choices made; follow-up meetings booked |
| B2B plays | Astronomer, Slack, Anthropic, Staples | A pre-approved response to sudden attention, a sign-up behind every big spend, a "we will not" line, or a hard-versus-easy contrast for sales | Registrations per moment; share of sales calls that repeat the line |

A mechanic only happens if the launch plan names who runs it and when. My [product launch communication plan](/blog/product-launch-communication-plan/) covers the order in which sales, customers and the public hear about it, and that order decides whether a stunt lands on a team that can answer the questions it creates.
