I treat each rupee figure for LinkedIn Premium as a store listing or a report, since none of them appears on LinkedIn's own price pages, and I compare them only as totals after GST. The app stores fold GST into the price they show. LinkedIn's GST FAQ applies 18% GST to all LinkedIn products, but its separate GST line on invoices covers purchases from LinkedIn India, which began selling to eligible businesses on 1 April 2026, and the FAQ says that part does not apply to LinkedIn Singapore. I read the ten as the items Apple's page displays rather than every item LinkedIn sells in the app, since Apple's listings for Tinder in the US and Candy Crush Saga in India also stopped at exactly ten rows that day. What the store listings tell you, and what they leave out:

## GST on LinkedIn Premium in India

The app stores include GST in the price they show, while LinkedIn's GST FAQ gives an 18% rate and, for invoices from LinkedIn India, a separate GST line. What each page says, as of 16 October 2026:

| Page | Its GST or tax wording |
|---|---|
| [LinkedIn India GST FAQ](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1456938) | "LinkedIn will charge GST at the rate of 18% to all LinkedIn products and services, beginning August 15, 2017." For supplies made through LinkedIn India: "The GST will be shown as a separate line item on your invoice, in addition to the product purchase price." The same section says: "This information does not apply to supplies made through LinkedIn Singapore Pte. Ltd." |
| LinkedIn's Premium product pages | Their dollar prices "may exclude ... Goods & Services Tax (GST)" |
| Google Play's India listing | "All prices include GST." |
| Apple's India listing | No tax wording |
| [Apple's 2023 developer talk on App Store pricing](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/110364/) | "Prices advertised to customers in all countries except the U.S. and Canada include taxes" |

The GST FAQ, which read "Last updated: 6 months ago" that day, also says:

- LinkedIn began selling through LinkedIn India Sales Private Limited on 1 April 2026, to eligible businesses in India. Customers currently buying from LinkedIn Singapore Pte. Ltd. "will continue to be billed by the Singapore entity until they re-purchase the services."
- "Non-enterprise customers: If you have a GST registration number in India, you may enter it in your billing information." From 1 April 2026, LinkedIn India issues e-invoices for business purchases, and a GST-registered buyer has to give LinkedIn its GST number during or before the purchase to claim the tax as an input credit, since "LinkedIn will not be able to update invoices previously issued."

## LinkedIn Premium App vs Web Price: Compare Totals After GST

Garg's February 2026 post set ₹1,850 a month on Google Play against ₹1,015.93 on desktop and called the difference an 82% markup. He puts the gap down to the platform fees Apple and Google take on in-app purchases, "15 to 30% of the transaction" in his words; LinkedIn's own price and help pages do not say so. Google Play's price includes GST by Google Play's own line, and the post does not say whether ₹1,015.93 does, so the gap depends on the reading:

| Reading of the desktop figure | Desktop total a month | App price a month | App costs more by | App costs more over 12 months |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ₹1,015.93 is the whole desktop charge (the post's arithmetic) | ₹1,015.93 | ₹1,850 | ₹834.07, or 82% | ₹10,008.84 |
| ₹1,015.93 is before 18% GST | ₹1,198.80 | ₹1,850 | ₹651.20, or 54% | ₹7,814.40 |

The second row is my arithmetic at the 18% rate in LinkedIn's GST FAQ: 1,015.93 x 1.18 = 1,198.80. LinkedIn's product pages put no figure on the gap and say only that "Slight variations may occur across device types."

## Is LinkedIn Premium Worth It in India?

LinkedIn Premium is worth it in India only if you will use a feature free LinkedIn lacks: InMail credits (5 a month on Career, 15 on Business, 30 on All-in-One), the 365-day viewer list, LinkedIn Learning's 25,000+ courses or the Company Page tools. Match the job to the plan before you look at any price:

- Job seekers who message hiring managers: Premium Career, for 5 InMail credits a month, the 365-day viewer list and LinkedIn Learning. What a free account already shows of your viewers is in [my comparison of search appearances and profile views](/blog/linkedin-search-appearances-vs-profile-views/).
- Finding clients: All-in-One where LinkedIn offers it ("Currently available to a limited group"), for prospect suggestions and 30 InMail credits a month, or Sales Navigator Core, which LinkedIn pitches at individual sellers.
- Growing a company Page: Company Page, for post-boost credits, follower invites and a custom testimonial.
- Reading your profile analytics by section: possibly Premium Business. LinkedIn's sample of the Profile engagement panel carries a Premium label and sits below a box reading "This feature is currently available only for Premium Business subscribers", as I found when working out [what impressions on your experience means](/blog/linkedin-impressions-on-your-experience/).

LinkedIn's Premium FAQ says a subscription "may be refundable within seven days from the date the subscription is charged without Premium usage", so a month you use counts as a paid month. With no rupee price on LinkedIn's public pages, the feature-against-cost comparison only works once you see your own checkout total.

On free routes, LinkedIn's free trial help page says not every member qualifies and that a trial converts to a paid plan unless you cancel before it ends, and LinkedIn Help marks the Microsoft 365 Premium Career student offer as no longer available. Airtel's Insider page listed Super Duolingo and PocketFM as that month's perks, with no LinkedIn offer, when I opened it logged out on 16 October 2026.
