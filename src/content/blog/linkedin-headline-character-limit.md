The opening of the LinkedIn headline I rewrote on 25 September 2026, "Senior Product Marketer, B2B SaaS & AI | I write the brief, then I build it", runs to 75 characters, which is 5 to 15 more than the 60 to 70 that [AuthoredUp's guide to LinkedIn character limits](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-character-limit) says display in search results, connection requests and comment sections. LinkedIn's own figure for the profile headline is 220, published in a February 2025 post on [its Talent Blog](https://www.linkedin.com/business/talent/blog/product-tips/recruiters-with-eye-catching-linkedin-profile-headlines) that tells recruiters "You get 220 characters", so the LinkedIn headline character limit has a LinkedIn source, just not on any Help page I read.

I trust a LinkedIn limit only when a LinkedIn page prints it. On that rule, 220 is safe to write to, while the figures for how much of a headline shows before the cut come from guides, and no LinkedIn page I read gives one.

## What Is the LinkedIn Headline Character Limit in 2026?

**220 characters.** LinkedIn's Talent Blog says so (27 February 2025), as do Linked Helper (29 September 2026) and AuthoredUp (11 August 2026), but none of the 42 LinkedIn Help pages I read on 16 October 2026 prints it. LinkedIn's 200 is an ad headline cap; 120 is an old figure.

[Linked Helper's character limit guide](https://www.linkedhelper.com/blog/linkedin-character-limit/) is by Margarita Servar and AuthoredUp's by Ivana Todorovic. [Jobscan's headline guide](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/impactful-linkedin-headline-examples/) by Kelsey Purcell (published 18 November 2025, modified 14 September 2026) and [Derrick's headline guide](https://derrick-app.com/linkedin/profile/headline) (updated October 2026) also print 220, and Derrick adds 240 on mobile.

Older pages print 120: [a May 2017 article Natalie Joan MacLellan published on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/wondering-linkedin-character-limits-natalie-joan-maclellan), and [a November 2018 career-coach newsletter](https://www.brandcareermanagement.com/november-2018-updated-information-on-working-around-the-headline-character-count-limitation/) that gave 120 on the website and 220 in the mobile app. [Copper's headline examples](https://www.copper.com/resources/linkedin-headline-examples), dated 18 September 2025, still say "you've only got 120 characters".

I read the Help pages logged out from an Indian connection. They include [Edit the Introduction section on your profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a547248), which lists Headline among the fields you can edit, and [Edit the About section on your profile](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a553140), which calls the headline "the text below your name on your profile". Neither gives a number. LinkedIn's Help search returned an HTTP 400 error to those logged-out requests, so I worked through its topic pages and links instead. Two of the pages I read link to one on enhancing your headline with LinkedIn's Profile Writing Assistance, and that page returned a 404 logged out, so a page I could not read could still carry a number.

## LinkedIn Profile Headline vs Ad Headline Limits

The profile headline shares its name with LinkedIn ad fields, and on the 42 Help pages I read, every headline number belongs to an ad, as with the single image and text ad figures below:

| Headline | Limit | Where LinkedIn states it |
|---|---|---|
| Profile headline, "the text below your name on your profile" | 220 | The Talent Blog post of 27 February 2025, not a Help page |
| Single image ad headline | 70 to avoid truncation, 200 maximum | Single image ads advertising specifications; LinkedIn's [Advertise on LinkedIn specs page](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/ads/sponsored-content/single-image-ads-specs) lists 70 as a text recommendation, with no maximum |
| Text ad headline | 25, including spaces | Text Ads - Advertising Specifications |

Linked Helper's table prints the single image ad's "70 (max 200)" in its ads section, labelled Sponsored Content, below the profile headline's 220, and that label is the thing to check before you copy a number from any guide. The two 70s are separate figures too: LinkedIn's ad pages use 70 for ad headlines, while AuthoredUp's "60-70" and Derrick's "about 70" describe profile headlines.

## How Much of a LinkedIn Headline Shows Before the Cut

None of the LinkedIn pages I read gives a number for it. Four guides do, and they disagree on the number and on the places:

| Guide | Characters it says show | Where it says the cut happens |
|---|---|---|
| Resume Geni (July 2026) | 60 | "in search results, comments, and mobile views" |
| AuthoredUp (11 August 2026) | About 60-70 | "in search results, connection requests, and comment sections" |
| Derrick (October 2026) | About 70 | "in the mobile preview and in search results" |
| Jobscan (published 18 November 2025, modified 14 September 2026) | 80 | "mobile search results and recruiter preview cards" |

None of the four links a LinkedIn Help page for its figure. Set against them, my 75-character opening would be cut like this:

| Cut at | Whose figure | Shown | Past the cut |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 | Resume Geni; AuthoredUp's low end | Senior Product Marketer, B2B SaaS & AI \| I write the brief, | then I build it |
| 70 | AuthoredUp's high end; Derrick | Senior Product Marketer, B2B SaaS & AI \| I write the brief, then I bui | ld it |
| 80 | Jobscan | All 75 characters | Nothing of the opening |

That is arithmetic on the guides' numbers, not a screenshot of where LinkedIn cuts my headline. The role keyword ends at character 23 and the market, "B2B SaaS & AI", at 38, inside all four figures. The line after the separator, which I ran through [a feature, benefit and value test](/blog/feature-vs-benefit-vs-value/), fits whole only under Jobscan's 80.

LinkedIn's [page on profile appearances](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a7105642) counts an appearance wherever your name and headline show up across LinkedIn, which [my explainer on LinkedIn search appearances vs profile views](/blog/linkedin-search-appearances-vs-profile-views/) breaks down by place. If you are updating the profile photo at the same time, [a Gemini or ChatGPT prompt for an edited headshot that keeps your face](/blog/prompt-for-linkedin-profile-picture/) is in a separate guide.

## How Long Should Your LinkedIn Headline Be?

Up to 220 characters, with the role keyword inside the first 60. For the total, the guides give ranges: Jobscan suggests 160 to 200 characters "to avoid truncation and maximize keyword coverage", and Derrick suggests keeping the whole headline "between 180 and 200 characters".

LinkedIn's Talent Blog gives its recruiter readers different advice. Rather than "simply listing your job title (which is displayed below anyway)", it says to "put those 220 characters to good use by showing who you are and what you're all about". A headline can follow both pieces of advice by opening with the role keyword and spending the characters after it on that material.

I would not pad a headline to reach a range: the only profile headline figure on any of LinkedIn's own pages I read is the 220 cap, and neither Jobscan nor Derrick links a LinkedIn Help page for its range. Leave room after character 60 for the other job titles and terms people search for. LinkedIn's [page on the order of people search results](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a521944) says "Standard job titles may be boring, but they are what other members search for."

## Recheck the LinkedIn Headline Character Limit Each Quarter

LinkedIn updates these Help pages. The post page's stamp read "7 days ago" on my check, and the invitations page, the row the guides contradict, read "5 months ago". A Help page could also start printing a profile headline number, which would give 220 a Help page source.

Until then, the LinkedIn headline character limit of 220 rests on a blog post, and the figures for the cut rest on guides. Write to 220, keep the role keyword inside the first 60 characters, and treat any other limit missing from the first table as a guide's claim until a Help page prints it. Start by counting where the role keyword ends in your own headline.
