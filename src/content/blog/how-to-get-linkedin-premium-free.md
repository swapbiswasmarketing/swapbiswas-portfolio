If you have ever started LinkedIn's own free Premium trial, Adobe's offer of three free months of Premium Career is closed to you for good, and Airtel's, if Airtel lists it again, stays closed until 12 months after that trial, going by [Adobe's offer FAQ](https://community.adobe.com/announcements-327/faq-adobe-express-x-linkedin-premium-3-free-months-offer-1561646) and [LinkedIn's Airtel offer page](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a13920014) as I read them on 16 October 2026. LinkedIn's own answer to how to get LinkedIn Premium for free is [its free trial](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1355837), which needs a valid credit card and turns into a paid plan unless you cancel before it ends.

Where each route stood on 16 October 2026, read logged out from India:

- **LinkedIn's free trial:** offered on LinkedIn's Premium pages, though "Not all members qualify" and "Trial length may vary based on region or promotional terms".
- **Adobe Creative Cloud or Express Premium:** 3 months of Premium Career, claimed at adobe.com/benefits. Adobe's community manager wrote on 30 September 2026 that the offer "doesn't have a publicly listed end date at this time", and Adobe's FAQ says it "is available in all regions where LinkedIn operates".
- **Airtel's LinkedIn perk:** absent from [Airtel's Insider page](https://www.airtel.in/insider), and LinkedIn's Airtel page says members who already hold a code are "currently not eligible to redeem".
- **Airtel's free Adobe Express Premium, then Adobe's offer:** Adobe's terms cover Express Premium members, yet members who got Express Premium through Airtel report LinkedIn's "not eligible" error in an [Adobe Community thread](https://community.adobe.com/questions-329/p-airtel-user-unable-to-redeem-linkedin-premium-benefit-not-eligible-select-regions-error-1625601) that had 114 replies that day.
- **Headway:** 3 months of Premium Career for paid Headway subscribers "in locations where LinkedIn Premium is available", offered "from October 2026 for a limited period".
- **Microsoft 365 student bundle:** ended. LinkedIn Help says "new codes are no longer being issued".
- **US military community:** 12 months of complimentary Premium through career services at three US nonprofits, first come, first served.
- **LinkedIn for Journalists:** 12 months of Premium Career for working journalists whose application LinkedIn approves.
- **Premium Company Page trial:** open to eligible Super Admins of a free Company Page, which LinkedIn says you may still get "even if you've already tried Premium before".

The order you claim these in decides how many you can get. The wording on Adobe's and LinkedIn's pages points one way: any partner offer you qualify for first, LinkedIn's own trial last. Offers like these change without notice, so every status here carries the date I read it.

Which to take first is what two commenters asked in a [March 2026 r/IndiaCareers thread on the Airtel route](https://www.reddit.com/r/IndiaCareers/comments/1rhx8cf/found_a_way_to_get_linkedin_premium_3_months_for/): on 11 July 2026, whether the trial could come before Airtel's offer, and on 10 August, whether a trial would still be possible after it. Neither got a firm answer in the thread. The thread's opening post, in updates dated 2 and 7 July 2026, reports a member who had used a one-month trial getting the 3-month offer on a later try, and its author getting 3 months twice after a trial a year earlier; those are reports, not rules. Read together, the vendors' clauses settle the first question and narrow the second, from their wording alone:

1. A LinkedIn trial **at any time** closes Adobe's offer, under the any-tier, any-time bar in the table above. Adobe's community manager wrote on 30 September 2026 that its "new to LinkedIn Premium" restriction "is set by LinkedIn's trial eligibility terms", and LinkedIn's [trial eligibility page](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a7472546) agrees in its own words: a member who has used a free trial "may not be eligible for any further free Premium subscriptions".
2. A trial in the last **12 months** also closes Airtel's offer and your next trial. [The FAQ on LinkedIn's Premium homepage](https://premium.linkedin.com/) says that if you have "redeemed a trial for your profile within the last 12 months, you won't be eligible for another free trial just yet."
3. Paid Premium closes Adobe's offer for at least **12 months** on Adobe's FAQ, and LinkedIn's Adobe page, quoted above, may reach further back; it closes Airtel's for **7 years** and Headway's for **20 years**. A current subscriber who wants a promotion must first cancel "and then wait for the billing cycle to end (This may take up to 30 days)", per LinkedIn's [promotional offers FAQ](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a549341), and those bars then still apply.
4. A partner offer can close the next partner offer. LinkedIn's Airtel page calls Airtel's offer a "promotional subscription", and Headway's bar covers "a LinkedIn Premium subscription" in 20 years with no "paid" in front of it, so on the words, Headway's 3 months have to come before any other free or paid Premium. LinkedIn's Adobe page, quoted above, can cover a free promotion as well as a trial.
5. A partner offer may count as a used trial. LinkedIn's three trial pages name no partner offer as a bar, and eligibility "depends on several factors". But the promotional offers FAQ says that redeeming a promotional offer means following the prompts "to begin the free trial", and Adobe ties its own restriction to LinkedIn's trial eligibility terms (item 1), so a trial after a partner offer is possible on the text but not promised.

So a trial taken first costs you Adobe's offer outright and Airtel's for 12 months, on the vendors' own words. A partner offer taken first may itself count as a trial, which no vendor page settles, so the cost of that order is a risk rather than a written rule. None of this is a guarantee in either direction: Adobe's FAQ says "Adobe and LinkedIn run independent eligibility checks, so passing Adobe's check doesn't guarantee you'll pass LinkedIn's."

## LinkedIn Premium Free Trial: Start, Length and Cancel Rules

To start the trial, per LinkedIn's [Redeem your LinkedIn Premium trial](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a7469538) page:

1. Click the Me icon at the top of your LinkedIn homepage.
2. Select Settings & Privacy from the dropdown.
3. Click Account preferences on the left pane.
4. Under Subscriptions & payments, click Upgrade for Free.
5. Select a plan and follow the prompts, entering a credit card when asked.

The rules that decide whether the trial stays free:

- The trial has no fixed length. LinkedIn's three trial pages give no number of days, and the main one says only that trial length "may vary based on region or promotional terms". [LinkedIn's Premium Career page](https://premium.linkedin.com/careers/career) points you to its Try Career for free button "to check if you qualify for a free trial", so your own offer shows only after you sign in.
- The trial needs a credit card, and none of LinkedIn's three trial pages mentions UPI. Airtel's offer needs a payment mandate, while Adobe's FAQ and LinkedIn's Headway page mention neither a card nor a mandate.
- Start it in a desktop browser, since LinkedIn emails a reminder before the trial ends "for any subscriptions purchased via LinkedIn desktop site".
- LinkedIn's trial eligibility page sets trial billing, cancellation and renewal dates in UTC for everyone except US-based English-speaking members. IST runs 5 hours 30 minutes ahead of UTC, so a trial started between midnight and 5:30 am IST is dated the day before.
- Switching plans during the trial means "you'll lose the free trial benefits of the original plan", and "Your billing cycle will begin immediately on the day you switch plans".
- Cancelling ends the trial at once. [LinkedIn's cancellation page](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a545578) says "If you cancel a Premium free trial, the trial will end immediately, and Premium access will stop right away", and asks you to cancel "at least one day before your next scheduled billing date", so cancel near the end of the trial.
- If the trial has already converted to a paid plan, the cancel steps differ by whether LinkedIn, Apple or Google Play billed you, as set out in [my guide to ending a paid Premium plan](/blog/cancel-linkedin-premium/).
- If it converts before you cancel, the [Premium subscription FAQ](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a570070) says a subscription "may be refundable within seven days from the date the subscription is charged without Premium usage".

To judge the trial, compare it with what a free account shows: a viewer count and the three most recent viewers over 90 days, per [my breakdown of LinkedIn's profile view counts](/blog/linkedin-search-appearances-vs-profile-views/). If private browsing is the reason you want the trial, read [how LinkedIn private mode works on Premium](/blog/linkedin-private-mode/) before the trial clock starts, since two LinkedIn Help pages disagree on whether a Premium member browsing privately keeps viewer insights.

## Can You Get LinkedIn Premium Free as a Student?

LinkedIn and Microsoft listed no live student offer on the pages I read on 16 October 2026. LinkedIn Help lists the Microsoft 365 student bundle that included Premium Career as "(no longer available)", and LinkedIn's Premium home, Career and Career compare pages do not mention students.

The bundle and how it ended:

- Microsoft announced on 15 January 2026 that college students could get 12 months of Microsoft 365 Premium and LinkedIn Premium Career. An Independent Advisor on [Microsoft's Q&A forum](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5736250/linkedin-premium-through-student-365-account) quoted the bundle's FAQ on 23 January 2026: the LinkedIn sign-up email would come "within 7 days", and "there are limited quantities available of LinkedIn Premium Career as part of this promotion".
- [TechRadar reported on 24 January 2026](https://www.techradar.com/pro/microsoft-is-giving-away-12-months-free-microsoft-365-premium-and-linkedin-premium-career-subscriptions-worth-usd300-but-you-will-need-to-be-a-student-to-get-it) that Microsoft gave the bundle as "available for a limited time, expiring March 1, 2026".
- [LinkedIn's help page on the bundle](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a9954084), last updated 8 months ago: "The promotion has ended, and new codes are no longer being issued."
- On 16 October 2026 the address of Microsoft's 15 January announcement, a post on its Copilot blog, redirected to [Microsoft's college student pricing page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/college-student-pricing), whose US and India versions both offered "Students save 50% on Microsoft 365 Personal" and neither mentioned LinkedIn.

For a student in India, that leaves the routes open to anyone: LinkedIn's trial, Adobe's offer if you hold Creative Cloud or Express Premium, and Headway's if you pay for Headway.

## How to Get LinkedIn Premium for Free: Claim in This Order

If you are working out how to get LinkedIn Premium for free from more than one route, claim in this order, on the terms as they read on 16 October 2026:

1. If you pay for Headway, claim its 3 months first, since its bar counts any Premium subscription in 20 years. Adobe's offer may close after it, as the list on earlier Premium above explains.
2. If you hold Creative Cloud or Express Premium, open adobe.com/benefits before you go near LinkedIn's trial.
3. Check the Airtel app's Rewards & OTTs page, in case the LinkedIn offer is shown to your number.
4. Take LinkedIn's own trial last, from a desktop browser, and cancel at least one day before the billing date.
