Guides to Instagram ad cost in India hand you a rupee rate. "You can start with as little as ₹100 per day for Instagram ads in India", says [Young Urban Project](https://www.youngurbanproject.com/instagram-ads-cost-in-india/), and [Boss Wallah](https://bosswallah.com/blog/creator-hub/instagram-ads-cost-in-india-2026-how-much-should-you-actually-spend/) gives an average of ₹2 to ₹6 per click. Meta's own page on minimum budgets has no such number. It opens: "This article uses US dollars when referencing minimum budget requirements. Minimum budgets may vary by country and objective."

Meta sets the rules for what an Instagram ad can spend and leaves the price to an auction: how far a day can run over budget, what a week can total, how a cost-per-result bid sizes the budget, and how Indian accounts are billed and taxed. Every rupee rate per click or per thousand impressions in those guides is the publisher's own estimate or currency conversion, and two of those pages each print CPC ranges whose tops differ by 20 times or more. I would plan the budget on Meta's rules and read each rupee rate as one source's dated guess. Meta's pages quoted here were read on 5 November 2026, and Meta rewords them; prices and limits change.

Meta's help pages publish no rupee price for Instagram ads. You set a daily or lifetime budget; on a daily budget Meta can spend up to 175% of it on one day and no more than 7 times it in a Sunday-to-Saturday week. Meta India bills accounts with India as the business country in rupees, adding GST and TDS.

## The Minimum Budget for Instagram Ads in India Is Not Published in Rupees

Meta's [Best practices for minimum budgets page](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/203183363050448) says a minimum exists and describes how it is set, without giving a rupee figure. When it sets one, Meta considers "business vertical, budget type, buying type, bid strategy, optimization, currency and schedule". You should see an alert in Meta Ads Manager if your budget does not meet the minimum, and Meta's cost page adds: "Some types of advanced ads have a minimum spending requirement. You'll be informed of this during ad creation."

upGrowth's page says Ads Manager shows your rupee floor at setup. That is consistent with Meta's alert, and it means the floor you see belongs to your account, objective and settings, not to Instagram in general.

The budget rules Meta does publish, with my rupee arithmetic on Meta's dollar examples:

| Meta's rule | Meta's example | Rupee version (my arithmetic) |
|---|---|---|
| With a cost per result goal, "your daily budget should be at least 5 times the amount of your cost per result goal" | $5 goal, $25 daily budget | A ₹50 cost per result goal needs at least ₹250 a day |
| Lowering a lifetime budget: keep it at or above what you have spent so far, plus 10% of what you spent over the past 2 days | $300 spent, $100 of it in 2 days: at least $310 | ₹3,000 spent, ₹1,000 of it in 2 days: at least ₹3,100 |
| Cutting a daily budget late in the day: the system may already have spent past the new amount | $200 cut to $100 at 5pm | Cut ₹2,000 to ₹1,000 in the evening and that day's spend can already be over ₹1,000 |

## Instagram Ad Cost per Day and per Week: Meta's Caps in Rupees

Meta's [About daily budgets page](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/190490051321426) defines a daily budget as "a daily spending target over the course of a calendar week", running Sunday to Saturday. On days with better opportunities Meta "may spend up to 75% over your daily budget", and two hard limits sit above that: "For every day ending at midnight, spending won't be more than 175% of your daily budget. For every week ending Saturday at midnight, spending won't be more than 7 times your daily budget."

With ad set budget sharing turned on, Meta may share up to 20% of an ad set's flexible daily budget with other ad sets. Meta puts that at "up to 210% of your daily budget" on a day, and its example gives $840 a week on a $100 daily budget, which is 8.4 times. Applied to rupee daily budgets:

| Daily budget | Most on one day (175%) | Most in a Sun-Sat week (7x) | With budget sharing: day (210%) | With budget sharing: week (8.4x) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ₹200 | ₹350 | ₹1,400 | ₹420 | ₹1,680 |
| ₹500 | ₹875 | **₹3,500** | ₹1,050 | ₹4,200 |
| ₹1,000 | ₹1,750 | **₹7,000** | ₹2,100 | ₹8,400 |
| ₹2,000 | ₹3,500 | **₹14,000** | ₹4,200 | ₹16,800 |
| ₹5,000 | ₹8,750 | **₹35,000** | ₹10,500 | ₹42,000 |

These rows are arithmetic on Meta's percentages; Meta's examples are in US dollars. Three cases the table does not cover:

- **Starting mid-week.** Meta prorates the week and adds up to 25% of the daily budget. Its example: $50 a day from noon on Wednesday spends no more than $187.50 by Saturday. At ₹500 a day from noon on Wednesday, that is 3.5 days x ₹500 + ₹125 = ₹1,875.
- **Changing the budget mid-week.** The same formula runs on the new budget for the days left. Meta's example cuts $100 to $50 at the start of Wednesday for a $212.50 limit over the 4 days; cutting ₹1,000 to ₹500 the same way gives 4 x ₹500 + ₹125 = ₹2,125, plus whatever already ran Sunday to Tuesday.
- **Spend over the caps.** Meta says it "will consider refunds" for spend above 1.75 times the daily budget on a day or 7 times it in a week, "depending on the specific use case".

The daily budgets page gives no monthly multiplier. For a month, count full weeks: ₹500 a day across four Sunday-to-Saturday weeks can spend at most 4 x ₹3,500 = ₹14,000 without budget sharing. If the month's total matters more than an even daily pace, Meta's page describes a lifetime budget as useful "if you don't want to exceed a certain amount of overall spend". Google caps a campaign by day and by month instead, and the [Google Ads cost guide for India](/blog/google-ads-cost-in-india/) works through its multipliers.

## Boost From the Instagram App, instagram.com or Ads Manager

Meta's [Ways to advertise on Instagram page](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/793315701035887) starts with the account: "To advertise on Instagram, you need to create or switch to a professional (business or creator) account." It then lists five places to start an ad, and they differ in what you control:

| Where you start | What you control | Advantage+ features | Payment note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram app (boost) | Goal, audience, daily budget, duration; "audience and placement options are set automatically" | Not stated for the app | On iOS you pay with available funds, and the Apple fee note below applies |
| instagram.com (boost) | "Boosting here has the same features as boosting from the app" | Same as the app | Meta's suggested way to avoid the Apple fee |
| Your Facebook Page | Some placement and audience options; ads can run on Facebook and Instagram | "full Advantage+ features are only available in Ads Manager" | Not covered on Meta's page |
| Meta Business Suite | Posts, ads, permissions and insights in one place | "Some Advantage+ features" | Not covered on Meta's page |
| Meta Ads Manager | Objective, budget, schedule, audience, placements, creative | "all Meta Advantage+ features that are available to you" | Not covered on Meta's page |

Meta's [boost instructions](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1067656009937668) run in this order:

1. Go to your profile and tap Professional dashboard, then Ad tools.
2. Tap Choose a post, pick a post or story, and tap Next.
3. Connect a Facebook Page when prompted, or tap Skip; if you skip, Meta says you won't see this step on future boosts.
4. Set the Goal (profile visits, website visits or messages), Audience, Budget per day and Duration.
5. Tap Boost post under Review. The ad runs after Meta reviews and approves it.

In [Ads Manager](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1513393428972189), you click + Create, pick an objective that supports Instagram, and set budget, schedule, audience and placements. To run only on Instagram, select Show more settings and pick Instagram under Platforms; Meta recommends Include all available placements instead.

The iPhone app carries a cost note on both Meta pages: "In most regions, an Apple service fee of 30% of your total ad payment, before any taxes and local fees, may apply when you add available funds to your ad account in the Instagram iOS app." Meta's advice is to "use instagram.com instead of the Instagram iOS app". Meta does not say whether India is one of those regions. Its India [TDS refund page](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/571983419976170) adds a second point for Indian businesses: Meta ads bought through the Apple App Store "are not eligible for a tax deduction at source (TDS) refund claim". Boosting from instagram.com, the route Meta names, keeps the spend off the App Store. Meta Verified is a separate subscription and not ad spend; its [rupee prices on the App Store](/blog/meta-verified-price-in-india/) are a different bill.

## GST and TDS on Instagram Ads in India

Meta's [India GST page](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1273346946097087) says that beginning October 2018, Meta ads in India "will be sold by Meta India, billed and paid for in Indian rupees and subject to a goods and services tax (GST) and tax deducted at source (TDS)", for every ad account with India as the business country. What the India pages say, as of November 2026:

| Item | What Meta's page says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| When tax is added | "GST and TDS are added whenever you're charged for your ads" | GST page |
| GST rate | Not stated on Meta's GST pages | GST page, [monthly invoicing GST page](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/242684093065691) |
| GSTIN | "Entering a GSTIN isn't mandatory"; enter it to take input tax credit | GST page |
| GSTIN format | A 15-character number, added under Business info in Payment settings | [Add your GST registration number](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1848545052064215) |
| TDS rate | "the current rate for advertising is 2%" | TDS refund page |
| TDS refund | For card and local payments: paid as Meta ad credit, applied to the ad account; "It may take up to 2 months" | TDS refund page |
| TDS deadline | "All certificates must be received by October 31 for the previous fiscal year" | TDS refund page |
| TAN | Must be added to each ad account to claim TDS back | TDS refund page |
| App Store purchases | Not eligible for a TDS refund claim | TDS refund page |

The 31 October deadline is for card and local payments; monthly-invoicing accounts pay TDS every quarter and send certificates as soon as they can; Google runs its own forms and quarterly deadlines, covered in the [Google Ads TDS refund guide](/blog/google-ads-tds-refund/). Meta's pages add that Meta "isn't able to give tax advice", so the GST rate on your invoice is a question for your accountant, not this table.

## How to Pay for Instagram Ads in India: Cards, UPI and Netbanking

Meta's [accepted payment options page](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/212763688755026) lists two groups for India:

- **Cards:** credit cards or co-branded debit cards, including American Express, Mastercard and Visa.
- **Local manual methods:** Netbanking and UPI, available "if the country of your ad account is set to India and your currency is set to the Indian rupee (INR)", and only "if you choose one when you first set up your ad account".

The first payment method also decides how you are charged:

| Payment setting | How it works | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Automatic payments | A card whose bank supports recurring payments; you are charged each time costs reach a payment threshold that starts small and may rise. Thresholds in India "may differ due to eMandate regulations enacted by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI)" | Payment options page; [About payment thresholds](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/776240779095515) |
| Available funds | UPI and other manual methods; you add money first and Meta deducts "up to once a day", with no payment threshold and an optional auto reload | [About available funds](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/173319523214585) |

The setup-only rule for UPI is the one to get right on day one: an account created with a card cannot add UPI later, per Meta's wording. Google's prepay and postpay settings work on a different logic, mapped in [Google Ads payment methods in India](/blog/google-ads-payment-methods-india/).

## Setting an Instagram Ad Budget in India From Meta's Rules

Working only from what Meta publishes:

1. **Pick the weekly amount you can lose** while you learn, then divide by 7 for the daily budget: ₹3,500 a week is ₹500 a day.
2. **Check the 175% day.** At ₹500 a day, one day can reach ₹875; turn on budget sharing and it can reach ₹1,050.
3. **Use a lifetime budget for a fixed total**, such as a sale that must not cost more than a set amount.
4. **Size the budget to your bid.** With a ₹50 cost per result goal, the daily budget should be at least ₹250.
5. **Boost from instagram.com**, not the iOS app, to stay clear of the Apple fee and of the App Store exclusion from TDS refunds.
6. **Decide UPI or card at setup**, and add your GSTIN if you want input tax credit.

The Instagram ad cost an agency quotes is its estimate from its own campaigns or a currency conversion. Meta's rules are the part you can plan on today. Check Ads Manager's estimate for your own budget before you trust any rupee rate.
