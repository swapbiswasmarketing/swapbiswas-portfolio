Microsoft Learn, read on 25 October 2026, says Copilot Chat includes "models (like GPT-5)" and that "by default, Copilot uses a real-time router to adjust the underlying model it uses based on your prompt". Both assistants offer OpenAI models, and Microsoft now offers GPT-6 and Claude models to licensed Copilot users too, which leaves three things to weigh in Copilot vs ChatGPT: where each assistant lives, what data it can see and what it costs.

That last part depends on which of the seven Copilot rows in the table below you mean. Asking whether Copilot beats ChatGPT has no single answer, in my view, until you name which Copilot: the free app, a Microsoft 365 home plan, the chat your employer's license includes, or a paid add-on. Each one maps to a different ChatGPT plan, at a different price.

## Copilot vs ChatGPT in One Answer

Copilot is Microsoft's assistant and ChatGPT is OpenAI's. Microsoft's work plans offer OpenAI's GPT-5.X, and Microsoft is adding GPT-6 Sol, GPT-6.1 Sol and Claude models for licensed users in phases. The paid license brings Copilot into Word, Excel, Outlook and Teams, grounded in your email, meetings and files. ChatGPT is a standalone app on GPT-6. If your work lives in Microsoft 365, start with Copilot.

## Is Copilot the Same as ChatGPT?

No. Copilot and ChatGPT are separate products from separate companies, sold and billed separately, though Microsoft's Copilot plans run OpenAI models.

Here is what each vendor's own page says about the models, as read on 25 October 2026:

To tell which Copilot you already have at work, read the label in the app. Microsoft Learn says "Copilot Chat (Basic)" means no add-on, and its users "don't have access to Copilot in Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and OneNote"; "Microsoft 365 Copilot (Basic)" means standard access in those apps without the add-on; and "Microsoft 365 Copilot (Premium)" or "(Pro)" means the add-on license with priority access.

## Is Copilot Free?

Yes, with limits, in two forms. A personal Microsoft account gets a free tier, and Microsoft's [plan comparison](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/buy/compare-all-microsoft-365-products) describes paid Personal as "Advanced Copilot chat with higher usage than free". At work, Microsoft Learn says "Using Copilot Chat doesn't require a Microsoft 365 Copilot license", because Copilot Chat (Basic) is "included with eligible Microsoft 365 licenses".

What the free and included tiers leave out, per Microsoft's pages:

- Volume. The free account's usage is listed as "Limited", and Copilot Chat's access to "file upload, image generation, and models (like GPT-5)" is "subject to service capacity availability".
- Your work data. Without a Copilot license, Copilot Chat "doesn't provide the broad Microsoft 365 organizational grounding that's available through Work IQ", except inside supported Outlook experiences.
- Copilot in the desktop Office apps. For home users it starts at Microsoft 365 Personal; Microsoft's footnote says "AI features are not available in Microsoft 365 Basic", its cheaper storage plan, which is a different thing from Copilot Chat (Basic).

Microsoft's US and India individuals pages both advertised a one-month free trial of Microsoft 365 Premium on 25 October 2026. ChatGPT's free plan is $0 too and runs GPT-6 Luna. ChatGPT Go is not free for new sign-ups in India any more, and [the end of the free Go year](/blog/chatgpt-go-free-india/) has its own post.

- Yearly billing saves money on Microsoft's side. Premium at ₹1,999 a month is the same monthly figure as ChatGPT Plus in India, which is billed monthly only; my [rupee price list for every ChatGPT plan](/blog/chatgpt-subscription-price-in-india/) has the rest. Premium's yearly price undercuts twelve Plus payments, and it adds the Office apps and 2 TB of storage.
- Personal includes "1 TB of secure cloud storage" alongside the Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and OneNote desktop apps, per the India comparison page.
- Family's AI is not shared. Microsoft's India FAQ says the AI features in Family, Premium and Pro "are only available to the subscription owner and cannot be shared with others."
- I found no GST or tax wording on Microsoft's en-in comparison page on 25 October 2026.
- Microsoft 365 Pro is sold in India: the India individuals page lists it at ₹9,999 a month, monthly billing only.

## Copilot or ChatGPT for Work: What Each Can See

Microsoft Learn's [Copilot Chat overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/overview) sets out grounding by experience:

| Experience | Grounding, in Microsoft's words |
|---|---|
| Copilot Chat (web or app) | "Web + user-provided content + supported app context" |
| Copilot Chat in Outlook | "Inbox, calendar, meetings, chats, and limited related content" |
| Microsoft Copilot licensed experience | "Graph API, Work IQ + semantic indexing across Microsoft 365" |

With the license, Microsoft says Copilot can reason across emails, meetings, calendars, chats, documents, sites and files that the user already has permission to open, and it "Doesn't grant access to additional organizational content." Since both sides now offer GPT-6 models to paying work users, I would weigh data access before model quality when choosing for work.

On the ChatGPT side, OpenAI's pricing page lists for Business "No training on your business data by default" and "A secure, dedicated workspace with essential admin controls, SAML SSO, and MFA". Microsoft's [comparison page against ChatGPT Enterprise](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/resources/compete-copilot-vs-chatgpt-enterprise) argues that Microsoft 365 data reached through ChatGPT connectors "is processed outside the Microsoft 365 service boundary". That is one vendor's claim about a rival, and the page's footnotes describe its comparisons as simulated videos captured between 19 December 2025 and 17 August 2026.

The comparison stops being a vague question once you name which Copilot you mean: the included work chat, a home plan, or a paid add-on, and each pairs with a different ChatGPT plan at a different price. Microsoft's Learn pages and its pricing pages use different names for the same add-ons, and its plan pages lag its model announcements, so the tables above are dated to the day I read them, 25 October 2026.
